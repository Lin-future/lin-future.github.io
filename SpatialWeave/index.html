SpatialWeave

Co-Evolving Tools, Workflows, and Skills for Spatial Reasoning

The model stays frozen. Its external harness evolves: tools that see, workflows that compute, and skills that say how to read the results. Evolved once with GPT-5.6 Sol, the same harness lifts every backbone we tried on seven spatial benchmarks.

    Across seven benchmarks and three frozen backbones, all 21 benchmark–backbone pairs improve, by 10.13 to 12.26 points overall. Qwen3.8-27B with the harness reaches 80.73, above GPT-6 Astra at 80.28, and the harness applied unchanged lifts Astra itself to 85.35.

    01Accuracy across backbones

    4,929 evaluation questions from seven spatial benchmarks. Bars start at zero. Hatched rows are GPT-6 Astra, shown as a reference.

    02How the harness evolved

    Each version applies one operator to the harness around a frozen GPT-5.6 Sol. After the first perception tools, more tools barely help: five specialized tools add +0.20, while generic skills add +2.36 and compiling tool chains into three workflows adds +2.37.

    03Where the gains land

    Gain over each backbone's base model, in accuracy points. Every cell of the three evaluated backbones is positive.

    04What each part of the harness fixes

    Five recorded runs, each decided by a part a plain tool-calling agent does not have: an evolved rule, a guard, a compiled workflow, a route readout. On their own, the same frozen models miss these questions. Diagrams are drawn from the recorded numbers; the runs play on their own.

    05Leaner once evolution ends

    During evolution a procedure scaffold told the solver to compose tools broadly. With the recurring chains compiled into workflows, the scaffold comes off: accuracy holds and every cost falls.

    06Takeaway

    An external harness can carry spatial reasoning for a frozen model. Beyond a core perception toolset, the gains come from compiling recurring computation into workflows and from skills that bind and interpret it, rather than from adding more tools.
